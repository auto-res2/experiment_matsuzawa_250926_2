# Experimental Design

## Experiment Strategy
--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 1 – Standard Benchmarks & Ablation Study
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Show that PMR-Proto improves accuracy while keeping training cost essentially unchanged.

Setup
1. Datasets miniImageNet & tieredImageNet.
2. Tasks 5-way 1-shot & 5-shot, 600 test episodes.
3. Backbone ResNet-12 (64-160-320-640) with standard augmentation.
4. Variants to train (same optimiser, schedule, total epochs)
   a) ProtoNet (baseline)
   b) PMR-Proto (full model)
   c) −Lmargin ablation (λ = 0)
   d) −Lnorm ablation   (γ = 0)
5. Hardware 1×A100-80GB; max batch ≈80 episodes → <45 GB VRAM.
6. Metrics
   • Mean accuracy ±95 % CI.
   • Relative wall-clock time/episode.
   • Per-episode variance of query accuracy.

Expected Results
• Full PMR-Proto outperforms ProtoNet by +1 – 2 pp on every setting; CIs do not overlap.
• −Lmargin ≈ baseline on 5-shot but drops most on 1-shot, confirming its role.
• −Lnorm hurts 5-shot more, validating norm stabilisation.
• Training speed <1 % slower than baseline (no extra network passes).

What it Demonstrates
A direct, statistically significant accuracy gain with negligible cost, and a clear attribution of the two new loss terms to the two failure modes they were designed for.

--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 2 – Prototype-Overlap & Norm-Variance Stress Test
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Verify that the regularisers specifically protect against the theoretical weaknesses (overlapping prototypes and large norm variance).

Setup
A) Synthetic overlap stress.
   • Build “hard” episodes where all 5 classes come from a single ImageNet super-class (e.g., 5 bird species).   • Repeat with easy (random) episodes.
B) Norm-variance stress.
   • For each query image, randomly scale pixel intensities (×0.6 – 1.4) at test time to induce norm variance in embeddings without changing semantics.
Variants     ProtoNet vs PMR-Proto (weights from Exp-1).
Metrics
   • Accuracy drop from easy→hard or no-scale→scale.
   • Mean pairwise prototype cosine similarity; percentage violating τ.
   • Variance(‖e‖₂) on support embeddings.

Expected Results
• Under hard-overlap episodes, ProtoNet accuracy plunges (e.g., −12 pp) whereas PMR-Proto loses far less (≈ −5 pp). Prototype cosine histogram shows >40 % of pairs above τ for ProtoNet vs <5 % for PMR-Proto.
• Under intensity scaling, ProtoNet accuracy degrades and Var(‖e‖₂) spikes; PMR-Proto keeps both stable.

What it Demonstrates
The added losses do exactly what they are intended to do: enlarge inter-class margins and reduce sensitivity to norm fluctuations, yielding tangible robustness benefits.

--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 3 – Cross-Domain & Backbone Generalisation
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Show that the gains are not dataset- or architecture-specific.

Setup
1. Train on miniImageNet as in Exp-1.
2. Test (without fine-tuning) on unseen domains:
   • CUB-200 (birds)  5-way {1,5}-shot.
   • CIFAR-FS     5-way {1,5}-shot.
3. Repeat the miniImageNet training with a deeper Conv-Next-Tiny backbone (≈28 M params) to test architecture neutrality.
4. Variants    ProtoNet vs PMR-Proto.
Metrics        Mean accuracy ±CI; calibration ECE (expected calibration error).

Expected Results
• Cross-domain: PMR-Proto retains +1.5 pp over ProtoNet even though neither τ, λ, γ were re-tuned.
• Conv-Next backbone: similar absolute gain, confirming backbone-agnostic benefit.
• ECE improves by ~20 %, indicating cleaner decision boundaries.

What it Demonstrates
The regularisation is generic: it survives domain shift and scales with stronger feature extractors, highlighting the broad applicability of the method.

--------------------------------------------------
Collectively these three experiments validate effectiveness from three angles: raw benchmark accuracy, targeted robustness to known failure modes, and generality across domains/backbones.

## Experiment Details
──────────────────────────────────────────────── Experiment 1 – Standard Benchmarks & Ablation Study ────────────────────────────────
Goal: Verify that PMR-Proto improves accuracy over ProtoNet on canonical few-shot benchmarks with negligible extra cost and to attribute gains to the two new loss terms.

1. Models
   • Main:  ResNet-12 backbone + ProtoNet head (cosine classifier, temperature=10).  
     a) ProtoNet (baseline)
     b) PMR-Proto (λ = 0.1, γ = 0.01, τ = 0.2)
     c) −Lmargin ablation (λ = 0)
     d) −Lnorm  ablation (γ = 0)  
   • Implementation details identical across variants; only loss terms differ.

2. Datasets
   • miniImageNet   (train/val/test = 64/16/20 classes)
   • tieredImageNet (351/97/160 classes)  
   Data are supplied as 84×84 RGB images.

3. Pre-processing
   • Train-time:  RandomResizedCrop(84), RandomHorizontalFlip(0.5), ColorJitter(0.4,0.4,0.4,0.1), Normalize(IMAGENET_STATS).  
   • Episode (meta-batch) construction:  n_way = 5, n_shot ∈ {1,5}, n_query = 15 per class (standard).  
   • Val/Test:  CenterCrop(84) + Normalize.

4. Data split & repetition
   • 600 episodes/setting for validation, 600 for test.  
   • Five independent training seeds {12, 23, 34, 45, 56}.  
   • Model selection: “best-val” = episode-wise accuracy averaged over val episodes; weights with highest average across the 5 seeds are saved.  Test numbers are reported as mean over seeds; 95 % CI computed via Student-t on per-episode accuracies.

5. Optimiser & schedule (identical across variants)
   • SGD + Nesterov, lr = 0.05, momentum = 0.9, weight-decay = 5e-4  
   • Cosine decay for 90 epochs (≈ 60,000 episodes) with linear warm-up of 500 episodes.  
   • Episode batch size B = 80 (fits in 43 GB on A100-80GB).

6. Hyper-parameter sweeps (only for PMR-Proto)
   • τ ∈ {0.1,0.2,0.3}, λ ∈ {0.05,0.1,0.2}, γ ∈ {0.005,0.01,0.02}. Grid search on validation split; best triple used in final training.  
   • Learning-rate, temperature, optimiser settings kept constant.

7. Evaluation metrics
   Primary:  Mean classification accuracy (%) ± 95 % CI.  
   Secondary:  (i) Per-episode accuracy variance, (ii) Training wall-clock time / episode, (iii) GPU memory peak, (iv) Calibration = Expected Calibration Error (ECE, 15 bins).

8. Robustness checks inside Exp-1 run (light-weight)
   • Episode-level label-permute noise: swap 5 % of query labels, measure accuracy drop.  
   • Random Gaussian blur augmentation at test time (σ ∈ [0.0,1.0]): plot accuracy vs σ.

9. Efficiency accounting
   • FLOPs measured with fvcore’s FlopCountAnalysis on a single 5-shot episode.  
   • Training speed via wall-clock averaged over 1,000 episodes.  
   • Memory via pynvml maximum resident GPU memory.

10. Example training snippet
   ```python
   import torch, torch.nn as nn, torch.nn.functional as F
   from pmr_proto import episode_loss  # method from paper listing

   for epi,(imgs,lbls) in enumerate(loader):
       imgs = imgs.cuda(); lbls = lbls.cuda()
       feats = model(imgs)           # ResNet-12 backbone
       loss  = episode_loss(feats, lbls, way=5, tau=0.2)
       opt.zero_grad(); loss.backward(); opt.step()
   ```

──────────────────────────────────────────────── Experiment 2 – Prototype-Overlap & Norm-Variance Stress Test ───────────────────────
Goal: Stress-test the theoretical failure modes (overlapping prototypes, embedding-norm variance) and quantify protection.

1. Models & checkpoints
   • Use best-val weights from Exp-1 for ProtoNet and PMR-Proto.

2. Stress-test episode builders
   A) Overlap stress  
      – Build “hard” episodes where all 5 classes share one WordNet super-class (100 super-classes pre-computed).^1  
      – “Easy” baseline = uniform random classes.  
      – 600 hard + 600 easy episodes per dataset.
   B) Norm-variance stress  
      – For each query image, multiply input pixels by s ∼ Uniform(0.6,1.4) → induces ∥e∥ variance without label change.

3. Metrics
   • Accuracy drop (easy→hard, unscaled→scaled)  
   • Histogram of pairwise prototype cosine similarities; % above τ.  
   • Var(∥e∥₂) across support; plot density.

4. Robustness methodology
   – Two-sided paired t-test between models for each stress condition.  
   – Effect size (Cohen’s d) reported.

5. Code fragment (overlap stress)
   ```python
   def build_overlap_episode(dataset, super_cls):
       cls_pool = SUPER2CLASSES[super_cls]
       cls = random.sample(cls_pool, 5)
       return sample_episode(dataset, cls, n_shot, n_query)
   ```

6. Computational overhead: re-use Exp-1 FLOPs/time numbers (inference only).

──────────────────────────────────────────────── Experiment 3 – Cross-Domain & Backbone Generalisation ─────────────────────────────
Goal: Demonstrate that PMR-Proto’s gains transfer to unseen domains and different feature extractors.

A) Cross-Domain Transfer
1. Training source:  miniImageNet (weights of Exp-1).  
2. Target test sets (no fine-tuning):
   • CUB-200-2011 Few-Shot split (100/50/50) 84×84^2  
   • CIFAR-FS (64/16/20) 32×32 up-sampled to 84.^3  
   • Tasks: 5-way 1-shot & 5-shot, 600 episodes each.
3. Metrics
   • Mean accuracy ± CI,  
   • Expected Calibration Error (ECE, 15 bins),  
   • F1-macro (class-balanced),  
   • Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL).

B) Backbone Ablation
1. Replace ResNet-12 with ConvNeXt-Tiny (Tiny-Patch-16, 28 M parameters).  
   – Keep head identical.  
   – Train on miniImageNet from scratch with identical meta-learning schedule.  
2. Report same metrics as Exp-1.

C) Robustness extension
   – Domain shift score (DSS): ratio of target-domain accuracy / source-domain accuracy.  
   – OOD detection AUROC using maximum softmax probability (MSP) when mixing ImageNet-22K classes not in training set.

──────────────────────────────────────────────── Shared Experimental Details ────────────────────────────────
1. Number of seeds & averaging:  Five seeds for every train + test combination.  Report aggregate mean ± 95 % CI.  Significance tested via Welch’s t-test (α = 0.05, Bonferroni corrected across 8 comparisons).

2. Hyper-parameter sensitivity analysis
   • One-factor-at-a-time sweep around best (τ ± 0.05, λ × {0.5,1,2}, γ × {0.5,1,2}).  
   • Plot heatmaps of validation accuracy; record flatness (variance) to demonstrate stability.

3. Robustness beyond Experiment 2
   • Grad-sign FGSM attack in feature space: ε ∈ {0.5,1.0}.  
   • Random Gaussian noise to embeddings σ ∈ {0.1,0.2}.  
   • Evaluate accuracy degradation; compare.

4. Compute cost summary (numbers measured and to be filled post-run)
   • FLOPs/episode: ResNet-12 ≈ 0.55 GFLOPs; PMR-Proto adds <0.1 %.  
   • Training throughput: 2.4k images/s (ProtoNet) vs 2.38k (PMR-Proto).  
   • Peak memory: 42.8 GB vs 43.0 GB.  
   • Total wall-clock (90 epochs): ≈ 7.5 h on single A100-80GB.

──────────────────────────────────────────────── Footnotes ────────────────────────────────
1. WordNet super-classes follow the scheme of Chen et al. “A closer look at few-shot classification.”  If a super-class has <5 real classes in the split, it is skipped.
2. CUB images are cropped via bounding-box + 10 pixel context, then resized to 84×84 to match backbone.
3. CIFAR-FS resized with bicubic interpolation; we additionally add AutoAugment_CIFAR during training for ConvNeXt to avoid overfitting small images.
