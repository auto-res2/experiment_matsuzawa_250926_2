# Experimental Design
- Strategy: --------------------------------------------------
Experiment 1 – Standard Benchmarks & Ablation Study
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Show that PMR-Proto improves accuracy while keeping training cost essentially unchanged.

Setup
1. Datasets miniImageNet & tieredImageNet.
2. Tasks 5-way 1-shot & 5-shot, 600 test episodes.
3. Backbone ResNet-12 (64-160-320-640) with standard augmentation.
4. Variants to train (same optimiser, schedule, total epochs)
   a) ProtoNet (baseline)
   b) PMR-Proto (full model)
   c) −Lmargin ablation (λ = 0)
   d) −Lnorm ablation   (γ = 0)
5. Hardware 1×A100-80GB; max batch ≈80 episodes → <45 GB VRAM.
6. Metrics
   • Mean accuracy ±95 % CI.
   • Relative wall-clock time/episode.
   • Per-episode variance of query accuracy.

Expected Results
• Full PMR-Proto outperforms ProtoNet by +1 – 2 pp on every setting; CIs do not overlap.
• −Lmargin ≈ baseline on 5-shot but drops most on 1-shot, confirming its role.
• −Lnorm hurts 5-shot more, validating norm stabilisation.
• Training speed <1 % slower than baseline (no extra network passes).

What it Demonstrates
A direct, statistically significant accuracy gain with negligible cost, and a clear attribution of the two new loss terms to the two failure modes they were designed for.

--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 2 – Prototype-Overlap & Norm-Variance Stress Test
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Verify that the regularisers specifically protect against the theoretical weaknesses (overlapping prototypes and large norm variance).

Setup
A) Synthetic overlap stress.
   • Build “hard” episodes where all 5 classes come from a single ImageNet super-class (e.g., 5 bird species).   • Repeat with easy (random) episodes.
B) Norm-variance stress.
   • For each query image, randomly scale pixel intensities (×0.6 – 1.4) at test time to induce norm variance in embeddings without changing semantics.
Variants     ProtoNet vs PMR-Proto (weights from Exp-1).
Metrics
   • Accuracy drop from easy→hard or no-scale→scale.
   • Mean pairwise prototype cosine similarity; percentage violating τ.
   • Variance(‖e‖₂) on support embeddings.

Expected Results
• Under hard-overlap episodes, ProtoNet accuracy plunges (e.g., −12 pp) whereas PMR-Proto loses far less (≈ −5 pp). Prototype cosine histogram shows >40 % of pairs above τ for ProtoNet vs <5 % for PMR-Proto.
• Under intensity scaling, ProtoNet accuracy degrades and Var(‖e‖₂) spikes; PMR-Proto keeps both stable.

What it Demonstrates
The added losses do exactly what they are intended to do: enlarge inter-class margins and reduce sensitivity to norm fluctuations, yielding tangible robustness benefits.

Experiment 3 – Cross-Domain & Backbone Generalisation
--------------------------------------------------
Goal: Show that the gains are not dataset- or architecture-specific.

Setup
1. Train on miniImageNet as in Exp-1.
2. Test (without fine-tuning) on unseen domains:
   • CUB-200 (birds)  5-way {1,5}-shot.

4. Variants    ProtoNet vs PMR-Proto.
Metrics        Mean accuracy ±CI; calibration ECE (expected calibration error).

Expected Results
• Cross-domain: PMR-Proto retains +1.5 pp over ProtoNet even though neither τ, λ, γ were re-tuned.
• Conv-Next backbone: similar absolute gain, confirming backbone-agnostic benefit.
• ECE improves by ~20 %, indicating cleaner decision boundaries.

What it Demonstrates
The regularisation is generic: it survives domain shift and scales with stronger feature extractors, highlighting the broad applicability of the method.

- Details: ──────────────────────────────────────────────── Experiment 1 – Standard Benchmarks & Ablation Study ────────────────────────────────
Goal: Verify that PMR-Proto improves accuracy over ProtoNet on canonical few-shot benchmarks with negligible extra cost and to attribute gains to the two new loss terms.

1. Models

     b) PMR-Proto (λ = 0.1, γ = 0.01, τ = 0.2)
     c) −Lmargin ablation (λ = 0)
     d) −Lnorm  ablation (γ = 0)  
   • Implementation details identical across variants; only loss terms differ.

2. Datasets
   • miniImageNet   (train/val/test = 64/16/20 classes)
   • tieredImageNet (351/97/160 classes)

3. Pre-processing
