TSE is inspired by theoretical connections between training speed and generalization, including stability-based generalization bounds for SGD, neural-tangent-kernel-inspired complexity measures, information-theoretic generalization bounds depending on gradient variance, and PAC-Bayes bounds under a Bayesian setting. The core estimator, TSE, is defined as the sum of mini-batch training losses over a specified number of epochs (area under the training curve). Two variants are introduced: TSE-E, which sums losses only from the E most recent epochs, and TSE-EMA, which uses an exponential moving average of training losses, assigning higher weight to later epochs (with a default decay rate of 0.999). The method focuses on training loss over validation loss, as validation loss can become unreliable due to overconfidence under misclassification in later training phases.
